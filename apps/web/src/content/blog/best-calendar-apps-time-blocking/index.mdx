The best time blocking app for most people is **Open Sunsama**. It is an open-source daily planner with time blocking that works with any AI agent. Your data stays yours, you can self-host it, and it has a public API.

We build Open Sunsama, so we put it first — here is why, and where others fit better.

**Akiflow** is best if your tasks come from many tools. **Motion** and **Reclaim** are best if you want AI to fill your calendar, and **Sunsama** is best for a calm, guided plan each morning.

For simple needs, a phone app like **TimeBloc** or **Google Calendar** with **Todoist** is enough. Below you get a comparison table, a short review of each app, and a guide to help you choose.

- **Best simple setup:** Google Calendar plus Todoist.

## How we compared these time blocking apps

In September 2026 we checked each app's website, pricing page, help docs, changelog and MCP or API docs. We also read hundreds of user reviews, plus threads on Reddit, Hacker News and Product Hunt.

We checked five things for every app:

- How fast you can turn a task into a time block.
- What happens when a day goes wrong and tasks roll over.

## AI auto-scheduling apps plan the day for you

These apps take your tasks, deadlines and meetings, then place the blocks for you. When a meeting moves, they move your tasks too. The trade-off is control: some people say the plan feels like a black box.

### 2. Motion: best for projects with deadlines

**Verdict:** Motion is the most hands-off time blocking app on this list.

**Best for:** Busy people and small teams who juggle many tasks with due dates.

**What works:**

- You add a task with a deadline and a length, and Motion finds the slot.
- When a meeting lands on your block, Motion moves the task for you.
- It also does projects, docs and team views, so some teams use it for everything.

**Limits:**

- The plan can shift a lot during the day, which some people find hard to trust.
- It does far more than time blocking, so setup takes a while.
- There is no free plan, only a short trial.

**Pick it if:** you would rather not decide when to do each task.

[Visit Motion](https://www.usemotion.com/)

### 3. Reclaim: best for protecting focus time and habits

**Verdict:** Reclaim guards your calendar more than it plans your to-do list.

**Best for:** People in meeting-heavy jobs who need to keep time for deep work.

**What works:**

- You set a weekly goal for focus time, and Reclaim books it around your meetings.
- Habits like lunch or a weekly review find the best open slot on their own.
- It pulls tasks from Todoist, Asana, ClickUp, Jira and Linear on paid plans.
- It connects to Claude over MCP and to ChatGPT.

**Limits:**

- It lives on the web, with no native phone app.
- It works on top of Google Calendar or Outlook, so you need one of those.
- Reclaim is now part of [Dropbox](https://reclaim.ai/blog/dropbox-acquires-reclaim), which some users watch with care.

**Pick it if:** your main problem is meetings eating the time you need for real work.

[Visit Reclaim](https://reclaim.ai/)

**Verdict:** Structured is a friendly timeline that mixes tasks and events.

**Best for:** People who want a light, pretty plan across phone and computer.

### 9. Google Calendar with Todoist: best simple setup

**Verdict:** A calendar you already know plus a strong task list covers the basics.

**Best for:** Light time blocking, or a first try before you pay for a planner.

## How to start time blocking in 15 minutes

The app matters less than the habit. Here is a simple way to begin in any time blocking calendar app.

1. **Brain dump.** List every task on your mind in one place.
2. **Pick three.** Choose the three tasks that matter most today.
3. **Guess the time.** Give each task a length, then add half again. Most of us guess low.
4. **Block around meetings.** Put your hardest task in your best energy hours.
5. **Leave gaps.** Keep about a third of the day open for surprises.
6. **Roll over, don't pile up.** At the end of the day, move what is left to tomorrow or the backlog.

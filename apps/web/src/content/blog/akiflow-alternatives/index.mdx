The best Akiflow alternative for most people is **Open Sunsama**. It is an open-source daily planner with time blocking that works with any AI agent. Your data stays yours: self-host it or use its public API.

We build Open Sunsama, so we put it first — here is why, and where others fit better. Pick **Sunsama** for a calm, guided daily plan. Pick **Morgen** if you want Akiflow's "all in one place" task hub with an AI planner that asks first.

- **Best overall:** Open Sunsama. An open-source daily planner with time blocking, a REST API and one MCP link for any AI agent.

## Why people leave Akiflow

Akiflow is good at one job: pulling tasks from Slack, email and your other tools into one inbox. Then you drag them onto your calendar with a few keys.

People who switch rarely say it is bad. They say one of four things.

- **Too expensive for a to-do app.** There is no free plan. Month to month, it costs more than Sunsama or Morgen.
- **The phone apps get mixed reviews.** In September 2026 the Android app had 3.7 stars on Google Play. Many 2026 updates fix crashes and sync.
- **The inbox fills faster than you can plan.** Capture from a dozen tools can turn into "a wall of stuff".
- **They want more control.** Some want a real auto-scheduler. Others want to own their data, with a public API or their own server.

Each app below fixes one of these. None fixes all four, so start with the problem that pushed you out.

## 2. Sunsama is the closest calm replacement for Akiflow

**Verdict:** the best Akiflow alternative if you want a slower, calmer day with a clear start and end.

**Best for:** people who time block by hand and want help not overplanning.

Sunsama looks a lot like Akiflow. Tasks sit on the left, your calendar on the right, and you drag one onto the other.

The difference is the ritual. Each morning it walks you through what you did yesterday and what fits today.

**What works**

- Guided daily planning and a shutdown ritual that ends the day on time.
- A workload total that warns you when the day holds more than you have hours for.
- Many task integrations, including Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Jira, Linear, Notion, Gmail and Slack.
- An AI assistant called Sunny and an [official MCP server](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/mcp-model-context-protocol), so Claude and ChatGPT can read and plan your day.

**Where it falls short**

- It is a little cheaper than Akiflow, but still a premium price. It went up in 2026 for the first time in five years.
- There is no public REST API. It has been the top request on Sunsama's roadmap since 2019.
- Some users find the daily ritual turns into a 20-minute chore.

**Pick it if** you liked Akiflow's layout but want a planner that helps you stop overplanning. We compare the two in depth in [Akiflow vs Sunsama vs Motion](/blog/akiflow-vs-sunsama-vs-motion).

## 3. Morgen is the closest task hub, with AI that asks first

**Verdict:** the best Akiflow alternative if you want every task and calendar in one view, plus an AI planner you approve.

**Best for:** people with many calendars and task apps who want help, not autopilot.

Morgen does what Akiflow does best: it brings tasks and calendars together. You can drag tasks onto the calendar by hand.

Or you can ask its AI Planner for a plan, then accept or change it. Nothing moves until you say yes.

**What works**

- Calendar sync for Google, Outlook, iCloud and Fastmail in one view.
- Two-way sync with eight task apps, including Todoist, Notion, Linear and ClickUp.
- An AI Planner that suggests a day and waits for your OK.
- Apps for Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS and Android.

**Where it falls short**

- No Slack or email inbox like Akiflow's, and no Asana, Jira or Trello sync.
- No free plan.
- Some reviewers call the phone app a companion to the desktop app.

**Pick it if** you want Akiflow's "all in one place" view with AI help that never surprises you.

## 4. Motion is the pick if you want AI to schedule for you

**Verdict:** the best Akiflow alternative if you are tired of dragging tasks and want software to place them.

**Best for:** busy people and small teams who trust an algorithm with their calendar.

Akiflow makes you place every block. Motion does it for you.

You add a task with a deadline and a length, and Motion finds a slot. When a meeting runs long, it moves the rest of your day.

**What works**

- Auto-scheduling that fills your calendar and fixes it when plans change.
- Projects, deadlines and team workloads in the same app.
- Apps for the web, Mac, Windows, iOS and Android, plus a public REST API.

**Where it falls short**

- There is no free plan, and the AI plans charge per seat.
- The schedule can feel like a black box. One user said you "have to reverse engineer how your own week got created".
- It takes real setup before the plans feel right.

**Pick it if** you want to stop planning and just follow the calendar. Our [Motion vs Sunsama](/blog/motion-vs-sunsama) post covers the trade-off in more detail.

## 5. Routine is a keyboard-first hub with notes built in

**Verdict:** the best Akiflow alternative if you love the command bar and want notes in the same place.

**Best for:** power users who plan from the keyboard and take meeting notes.

Routine feels close to Akiflow. You capture tasks from a command bar, see your calendar next to them, and drag to time block. It adds notes, so your meeting notes live next to the tasks they create.

**What works**

- A free plan, and paid plans from $5 a month.
- Tasks, calendar and notes in one app.
- Apps for every platform, including Linux, and a new AI assistant.

**Where it falls short**

- Routine itself says its phone apps lag behind the desktop app.
- Its MCP server only runs on your computer, with the desktop app open.

**Pick it if** you want Akiflow's speed with notes in the same app.

## 6. TickTick is a strong task list with a calendar view

**Verdict:** the best Akiflow alternative if you mostly need a great to-do list, not a full time-blocking workspace.

**Best for:** people on a budget who want one app for tasks, habits and a timer.

Some people leave Akiflow and realize they never needed a task hub. They needed a good list and a way to see it on a calendar. TickTick does that on every device.

**What works**

- Fast capture that understands "call Sam Friday 3pm".
- A calendar view, habit tracker, Pomodoro timer and Eisenhower matrix.
- Apps for every platform, plus an official MCP server for AI agents.

**Where it falls short**

- The full calendar view and the timeline need Premium.
- Few integrations. It won't pull tasks from Slack, Asana or Linear.
- Time blocking is basic next to Akiflow's.

**Pick it if** a solid list on your phone matters more than a calendar-first plan.

## 7. Ellie is the simplest visual planner

**Verdict:** the best Akiflow alternative if Akiflow feels like too much software.

**Best for:** people who want a pretty, light board and a calendar, and little else.

Ellie shows your days as columns, like a paper planner. You brain dump tasks, drag them to a day, then drag them onto a time. There is little to set up.

**What works**

- A clean board of days that is easy to learn.
- A free plan, and a one-time lifetime option.
- An AI assistant on Pro that can timebox your day, with a mode that asks first.

**Where it falls short**

- Fewer integrations, so it won't replace Akiflow's inbox.
- No Android or Linux app.

**Pick it if** you want the calm of a paper planner with drag and drop.

## How to switch from Akiflow without losing a week

Moving planners is where most people give up. Keep it small.

1. **Move only this week's tasks.** Leave the old backlog in Akiflow for a month. You will find you need very little of it.
2. **Connect your calendar first.** Your meetings are the frame. Tasks fit around them.
3. **Rebuild one habit at a time.** Start with the morning plan. Add a shutdown ritual in week two.
4. **Let an agent do the typing.** If your new planner has an MCP server, paste your task list into Claude or ChatGPT and ask it to create the tasks.
5. **Cancel once you have planned five real days.** That is enough to know if it sticks.

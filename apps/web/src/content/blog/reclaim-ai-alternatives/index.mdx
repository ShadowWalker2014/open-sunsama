The best Reclaim.ai alternative for most people is **Open Sunsama**. It is an open-source daily planner with time blocking that works with any AI agent. Your data stays yours, you can self-host it, and it has a public API.

We build Open Sunsama, so we put it first — here is why, and where others fit better.

Pick **FlowSavvy** if you want the same auto-scheduling with full phone apps. Pick **Motion** if you want even more done for you, including projects.

Pick **Morgen** for AI that suggests a plan and waits for your OK. **Sunsama** guides a calm manual plan.

## Key takeaways

- **Best overall:** Open Sunsama. An open-source planner that you, or any AI agent, can run. You own the data.
- **Closest Reclaim alternative for one person:** FlowSavvy. Auto-scheduling, full iOS and Android apps, and iCloud support.

## Why people look for a Reclaim.ai alternative

Reclaim is a smart layer on top of Google Calendar or Outlook. It books time for your tasks, habits and focus, then moves those blocks by priority when meetings land.

Many teams love it. People leave for a handful of reasons:

- **No phone apps.** Reclaim has no native iOS or Android app. Its help page suggests saving the web app to your home screen.
- **Google and Outlook only.** There is no iCloud calendar support.
- **The automation can feel opaque.** One Reddit user put it plainly: "I found the automation too opaque."
- **Task sync costs extra.** The free plan syncs Google Tasks. Todoist, Asana, ClickUp, Jira, Linear and Notion need a paid plan.
- **The product is changing.** Dropbox bought Reclaim in August 2024. Reclaim 2.0, now in private beta, suggests tasks instead of placing them on its own.

If the phone gap is your problem, look at FlowSavvy, Morgen or Motion. If trust is your problem, look at Open Sunsama, Morgen or Sunsama.

## What happened to Clockwise?

Clockwise shut down on March 27, 2026. Its team joined Salesforce to work on Agentforce, and the app stopped working that day. [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/03/20/salesforce-acquihires-team-behind-clockwise-for-agentforce/5226782) reported that user data was being deleted, and Salesforce gets no access to it.

Clockwise pointed its users to Reclaim. So if a 2025 list tells you to "try Clockwise", skip it. Every pick below is still live.

## How we compared these Reclaim alternatives

In September 2026 we checked each app's website, pricing page, help docs and changelog. We read how each one says it handles tasks with deadlines, daily habits and a meeting that moves.

We also read its MCP and API docs, plus hundreds of user reviews and threads on Reddit, Hacker News and Product Hunt. Reviews show what docs leave out, like how often blocks jump around.

We also build and use Open Sunsama every day.

We judged each app on four questions:

- **Does it protect time for tasks and habits without daily work?**
- **Can you see why a block moved?**
- **Can you plan from your phone?**
- **Can Claude, ChatGPT or another agent work with it?**

Every price and feature comes from the vendor's own site. We left out what we could not confirm there.

## 2. FlowSavvy is the closest Reclaim alternative for one person

**Verdict:** the best pick if you like Reclaim's auto-scheduling but want real phone apps.

**Best for:** solo users who want their week planned for them.

FlowSavvy schedules tasks into your calendar by priority and deadline. It re-plans when something changes. If that feels too jumpy, you can turn off auto-recalculate and re-plan with a button instead.

**What works**

- Full iOS and Android apps, with widgets.
- Two-way sync with Google, Outlook and iCloud, plus Todoist.
- A free plan that schedules up to two weeks ahead.
- An API and an MCP server on the Pro plan.

**Where it falls short**

- No team features or meeting scheduling like Reclaim's.
- No desktop apps. You use the web app on a computer.

**Pick it if** you want Reclaim's autopilot for yourself, on your phone too.

## 3. Motion does more for you than Reclaim

**Verdict:** the best Reclaim alternative if you want tasks, projects and your calendar planned in one app.

**Best for:** busy people and teams who are happy to let software run the day.

Reclaim guards time on your calendar. Motion goes further. You add tasks with deadlines and lengths, and it builds your whole day.

It re-plans when a meeting runs long. It also runs projects, docs and meeting notes.

**What works**

- Auto-scheduling for tasks and projects in one place.
- Apps for the web, Mac, Windows, iOS and Android.
- A public REST API for your own automations.

**Where it falls short**

- No free plan, and it costs more than Reclaim.
- The schedule can feel like a black box.
- No Linux app, and we found no official MCP server.

**Pick it if** you want more automation, not less. Our [Reclaim AI vs Motion vs Sunsama](/blog/reclaim-ai-vs-motion-vs-sunsama) post compares them side by side, and our [Motion alternatives](/blog/motion-alternatives) guide covers more options.

## 4. Morgen suggests a plan and waits for your OK

**Verdict:** the best Reclaim alternative if Reclaim's moves made you nervous.

**Best for:** people with many calendars and task apps who want AI help they can check.

Morgen shows every calendar and task list in one view. Its AI Planner proposes where tasks should go.

You accept, change or reject the plan. When a meeting lands on a task, it asks before moving anything.

**What works**

- Calendars from Google, Outlook, iCloud and Fastmail in one view.
- Two-way task sync with Todoist, Notion, Linear, ClickUp, Microsoft To Do and more.
- Native apps for Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS and Android.

**Where it falls short**

- It won't defend focus time on its own like Reclaim does.
- Fewer task sources than Reclaim: no Asana or Jira.

**Pick it if** you want AI to do the math and you to make the call.

## 5. SkedPal gives you the deepest scheduling rules

**Verdict:** the best Reclaim alternative if Reclaim felt too simple.

**Best for:** planners who want exact rules for when each task can run.

SkedPal uses "time maps", named windows like "weekday mornings" with rules attached. You can say "one hour this week, mornings only, 30 minutes a day at most". It builds the plan when you click "Update schedule".

**What works**

- Very fine control over time windows, splits and limits.
- Nested subtasks for big projects.
- You decide when the plan updates, so there are no surprise moves.

**Where it falls short**

- Reviewers call its learning curve the steepest in the group.
- The phone apps are companions, not full planners.
- No free plan.

**Pick it if** you want an auto-scheduler you can tune until it thinks like you.

## 6. Sunsama guides a calm manual plan

**Verdict:** the best Reclaim alternative if you want to choose your day, not have it chosen.

**Best for:** people who want a realistic day and a clear end to work.

Sunsama does not auto-schedule. Each morning it walks you through what fits today.

You drag tasks onto your calendar yourself, and it warns you when you plan too much. At night, a shutdown ritual helps you stop on time.

**What works**

- A daily planning ritual and a workload total that stops overplanning.
- Two-way sync with about 20 task tools, plus Google, Outlook and iCloud calendars.
- An AI assistant called Sunny and an [official MCP server](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/mcp-model-context-protocol) for Claude and ChatGPT.

**Where it falls short**

- No habits or focus-time defense like Reclaim's.
- No public REST API.
- Some users find the ritual turns into a daily chore.

**Pick it if** you want calm and control more than automation.

## 7. Google Calendar may be all you need

**Verdict:** the best Reclaim alternative if you only used Reclaim for focus time or booking links.

**Best for:** Google Workspace users with simple needs.

Google Calendar now does some of what people installed Reclaim for. Focus time blocks mute chat and decline new meetings. Appointment schedules give you a booking page.

**What works**

- Nothing new to install or learn.
- Focus time that auto-declines meetings.
- One booking page on a personal account, and more on paid plans.

**Where it falls short**

- You place and repeat every block yourself. Nothing moves when plans change.
- Focus time needs a Workspace plan such as Business Standard. Personal accounts don't get it.
- No task scheduling at all.

**Pick it if** Reclaim felt like more than you used.

The best open source focus app in 2026 is **Open Sunsama**, if you want to focus on the day you planned. It opens one task full screen with a timer against your estimate, so you see planned vs actual.

For an open source Pomodoro app, pick **Super Productivity** or **Pomatez**. For open source time tracking software, pick **Kimai** or **solidtime**.

We build Open Sunsama, so we put it first — here is why, and where others fit better.

Open Sunsama has no built-in Pomodoro cycle. Its timer counts up against your estimate, with no set sprints or forced breaks. If you want 25-minute sprints, the Pomodoro timers below do that better, and they run fine next to it.

## Key takeaways

- **Best overall:** Open Sunsama, for focusing on your planned day. Focus mode runs a timer against your estimate and shows planned vs actual.
- **Best open source Pomodoro app with tasks:** Super Productivity, on desktop and phone.
- **Best standalone Pomodoro timers:** Pomatez for strict sessions, Pomotroid for stats and themes.
- **Best break reminder:** Stretchly, which makes you stand up and rest your eyes.
- **Best open source time tracker for billing:** Kimai. solidtime is the modern pick for freelancers.
- **Simple self-hosted time logs:** Traggo and TimeTagger.
- **Automatic tracking:** ActivityWatch logs your apps and sites on your own computer.

## Open Sunsama is the best open source focus app for a planned day

<OssApp id="open-sunsama" rank={1} bestFor="Focusing on your planned day: focus mode with a timer against your estimate, and planned vs actual." />

**Verdict:** best overall. Focus mode lives inside your daily plan, so you always focus on a task you already chose.

Most focus apps start with a blank timer. Open Sunsama starts with your day. You plan tasks on a board, give each one an estimate, and block time for it on your calendar.

Then press F on a task. It fills the screen with its subtasks, notes and a focus timer. Press Space to start.

<Clip id="focus" caption="Press F to open a task in focus mode, then Space to start the timer." />

The timer counts up next to your estimate, like 0:42 / 1:30. When you stop, it saves the real time to the task.

<Shot id="focus" alt="Open Sunsama focus mode showing a task with 0:42 of 1:30 spent, its subtasks and notes" caption="0:42 spent of a 1:30 estimate, with subtasks and notes in view." />

At the end of the day, you check off what you did. Each done task keeps its real time next to its estimate. That is planned vs actual, and it makes tomorrow's plan more honest.

<Clip id="shutdown" caption="Check off tasks at the end of the day and see what you really got done." />

What it does well:

- Focus mode on any task, with subtasks and notes in view.
- A timer that counts up, so a long task never cuts you off.
- Planned vs actual for each task and each day.
- [Time blocking](/features/time-blocking) on a calendar next to your Google, Outlook or iCloud events.
- A hosted MCP server, so Claude or ChatGPT can read your estimates and actual time.
- Web, plus desktop apps for Mac, Windows and Linux.

Where it falls short:

- No built-in Pomodoro cycle, so no set sprints or forced breaks.
- No break reminders and no automatic app tracking.
- No iOS or Android app. On a phone, it runs in the browser.
- The code is public on GitHub. It uses a non-commercial license: you can read it, run it and self-host it for personal use. Companies need a commercial license.

**Pick it if** you want to focus on the work you planned, not just run a timer. Add Pomatez or Pomotroid if you also want Pomodoro sprints. Read more on the [focus mode page](/features/focus-mode).

## Open source Pomodoro timers and break reminders

A Pomodoro timer splits work into short sprints with breaks in between. Our [Pomodoro technique guide](/blog/pomodoro-technique-guide) explains the method.

### Super Productivity: a Pomodoro timer inside a full to-do app

<OssApp id="super-productivity" rank={2} bestFor="A Pomodoro timer, a to-do list and time tracking in one app, on desktop and phone." />

**Verdict:** the best open source Pomodoro app if you want your tasks and your timer in one place.

What it does well:

- A Pomodoro timer, a break reminder and time tracking for each task.
- Timeboxing, so you plan how long each task gets.
- Works offline with no account. Syncs through SuperSync, Dropbox or WebDAV.
- Pulls in issues from Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Linear and more.

Where it falls short:

- It has many features and settings, so it takes a while to learn.
- MCP comes from community servers, not the core app.

**Pick it if** you want a Pomodoro timer and a to-do list with real phone apps.

### Pomatez: strict Pomodoro sessions on your desktop

<OssApp id="pomatez" rank={3} bestFor="Strict Pomodoro sessions, with full-screen breaks you can't skip." />

**Verdict:** the best standalone open source Pomodoro timer for the desktop.

What it does well:

- You set the length of work, short breaks and long breaks.
- Strict mode stops you from pausing, skipping or resetting a session.
- Full-screen breaks keep you off the keyboard while you rest.
- Special breaks for lunch or a snack, set once at fixed times.
- A small task list, so you know what each session is for.

Where it falls short:

- Desktop only, with no phone app and no sync.
- The task list is basic. It does not plan your day.

**Pick it if** you want a timer that won't let you cheat.

### Pomotroid: a Pomodoro timer with stats and themes

<OssApp id="pomotroid" rank={4} bestFor="A good-looking Pomodoro timer that keeps a record of your sessions." />

**Verdict:** the best open source Pomodoro timer for people who like to see their streaks.

What it does well:

- You set work and break lengths, and the rounds before a long break.
- Daily, weekly and all-time stats, with a heatmap of the past year.
- 38 built-in themes, and you can add your own.
- Global shortcuts control the timer even when its window is hidden.

Where it falls short:

- No task list. It times sessions, not tasks.
- Desktop only, with no sync between computers.

**Pick it if** you want a simple timer and a chart of your focus time.

### Solanum: the simplest Pomodoro timer for GNOME

<OssApp id="solanum" rank={5} bestFor="GNOME users who want a tiny timer with one button." />

**Verdict:** a small, calm timer for the Linux desktop, now in maintenance mode.

What it does well:

- One button starts a work lap, then a break.
- It looks and feels like the rest of the GNOME desktop.
- It can go full screen during breaks.

Where it falls short:

- Linux only, from Flathub.
- It gets only small fixes and translations now. The last new feature landed in 2024.

**Pick it if** you use GNOME and want the least setup possible.

### Stretchly: break reminders, not a Pomodoro timer

<OssApp id="stretchly" rank={6} bestFor="People who sit too long and forget to take breaks." />

**Verdict:** the best open source app to make you rest.

What it does well:

- A 20-second mini break every 10 minutes and a 5-minute long break every 30 minutes. You can change all of it.
- It pauses breaks when you step away, or when Do Not Disturb is on.
- You can put a break off once, then skip it later if you must.
- A command line lets you control the running app.

Where it falls short:

- It does not time your work or track tasks.
- The macOS build is not signed, so macOS may block the first launch.

**Pick it if** your problem is sitting too long, not staying on task.

## Open source time tracking software

A time tracker logs how long work takes. Use one to bill clients, or to learn where your week goes. Our guide on [how to track your time](/blog/how-to-track-your-time) covers the habit.

### Kimai: time tracking with invoices

<OssApp id="kimai" rank={7} bestFor="Freelancers and teams who bill clients by the hour." />

**Verdict:** the most complete open source time tracker for billable work.

What it does well:

- Timesheets, reports, invoices and teams in one web app.
- Rates per user, customer or project, plus budgets and tags.
- An API, two-factor login and a plugin store.
- Self-host it with Docker, or on any PHP host.

Where it falls short:

- Some extra features are paid plugins.
- Phone apps come from other developers, not the Kimai project.

**Pick it if** you need timesheets and invoices on your own server.

### solidtime: a modern time tracker for freelancers

<OssApp id="solidtime" rank={8} bestFor="Freelancers and agencies who want a clean, modern timer for client work." />

**Verdict:** the most modern open source time tracker, built for client work.

What it does well:

- Projects, clients and billable rates.
- A timer on the web, in desktop apps and in a browser extension.
- A REST API, and Docker for self-hosting.

Where it falls short:

- Some advanced self-hosted features need a paid business plan.
- No phone app.

**Pick it if** you want a clean timer for client work that you can host yourself.

### Traggo: time tracking by tags

<OssApp id="traggo" rank={9} bestFor="People who want to tag their time freely instead of filing it under projects." />

**Verdict:** a small, flexible tracker built on tags, not tasks.

What it does well:

- You tag spans of time, like "project:blog" or "type:meeting".
- A calendar view and dashboards you build from your tags.
- A GraphQL API, and Docker or a single binary to run it.

Where it falls short:

- A small project with slow releases.
- No phone app.

**Pick it if** you want to slice your time any way you like.

### TimeTagger: your day as a timeline

<OssApp id="timetagger" rank={10} bestFor="People who like to see and drag their day on a timeline." />

**Verdict:** a light tracker built around a timeline you can drag.

What it does well:

- An interactive timeline you drag to see any stretch of time.
- Plain #tags in each entry, with reports by tag.
- A web API and a command-line tool.
- Self-host it with Docker or pip.

Where it falls short:

- New work comes slowly. The last commits were in May 2026.
- No phone app, though the web app works in a phone browser.

**Pick it if** you think about time as blocks on a line.

## Automatic time tracking

### ActivityWatch: automatic tracking that stays on your computer

<OssApp id="activitywatch" rank={11} bestFor="Seeing where your screen time really goes, with no timer to start." />

**Verdict:** the best open source automatic time tracker.

What it does well:

- It logs your active app and window title as you work.
- A browser extension adds the tab and site you are on.
- It knows when you step away from the keyboard.
- Your data stays on your computer, with a local REST API.

Where it falls short:

- Sync between devices is still in testing.
- It shows where time went, but not what you should work on.

**Pick it if** you want the plain truth about your screen time.

## What about open source habit trackers?

Most apps here track time, not habits. For a daily habit, a recurring task in Open Sunsama puts it on your board each day, and the focus timer logs the time.

If you want a streak chart, look at [Loop Habit Tracker](https://github.com/iSoron/uhabits). It is a well-known open source habit app for Android, under GPL-3.0. We did not rank it here because it does not time your work.

## Which focus apps work with AI agents?

MCP lets an AI agent like Claude or ChatGPT read and change your data. For focus apps, that means your agent can see planned vs actual and plan tomorrow.

- **Open Sunsama:** an official hosted MCP server with OAuth sign-in. Your agent can read estimates and actual minutes, and add time blocks. See the [MCP setup guide](/docs/mcp/overview).
- **Super Productivity, Kimai, solidtime, Traggo, TimeTagger and ActivityWatch:** community MCP servers built by other people.
- **Pomatez, Pomotroid, Solanum and Stretchly:** we found no MCP server.

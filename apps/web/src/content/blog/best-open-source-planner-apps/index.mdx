## A planner gives each task a day and a time; a to-do list only gives it a place

A to-do list holds what you need to do. A planner adds when. Time blocking goes one step further: each task gets its own slot on your calendar, next to your meetings.

Timeboxing is close, but not the same. You give a task a fixed amount of time, like 45 minutes, and stop when the time is up. Many apps here do both.

Why bother? A long list hides how little time you have. Blocks on a calendar show it, so you plan a calm, realistic day.

Our [time blocking guide](/blog/complete-guide-time-blocking) explains the method, and our [time blocking feature page](/features/time-blocking) shows how Open Sunsama does it.

## Super Productivity comes closest to an open source Motion

Motion and Reclaim place your tasks on the calendar for you. We found no open source app that does this for your whole day.

Super Productivity comes closest. Its Schedule view flows tasks around your meetings, using the time you estimate for each one. It shows the plan; it does not book time in your calendar.

Open Sunsama takes another route. You ask Claude or ChatGPT to plan the day over MCP, and it places the blocks where you can see and move them. Our [Motion alternatives](/blog/motion-alternatives) guide covers the paid auto-schedulers.

## MIT and AGPL let you self-host freely; Open Sunsama's license limits company use

The license tells you what you may do with the code. For one person running a planner at home, MIT, BSD, GPL and AGPL all give you full freedom.

| License | Apps in this list | Self-host for yourself | Change the code | Use at a company |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MIT, BSD** | Super Productivity, dayGLANCE, tududi, calcurse | Yes | Yes. Keep the copyright notice. | Yes |
| **GPL-3.0** | Planify | Yes | Yes. If you give the app to others, share your changes too. | Yes |
| **AGPL-3.0** | Leantime, Vikunja | Yes | Yes. If others use your changed copy, even over the web, share your changes. | Yes |
| **Non-commercial** | Open Sunsama | Yes, for personal use | Yes, for personal use | Needs a commercial license |

Here is Open Sunsama's license in plain words. The code is public on GitHub, under a non-commercial license.

You can read it, run it and self-host it for personal use. Companies need a commercial license. That is not an OSI license like MIT or GPL, so pick Super Productivity or Leantime if your company needs one.

Some apps also sell extras. Leantime sells paid plugins, and dayGLANCE charges for its store apps.

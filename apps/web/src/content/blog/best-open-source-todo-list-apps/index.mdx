The best open source to-do list app overall in 2026 is **Open Sunsama**. It keeps your to-do list, your calendar and your day plan in one place, and any AI agent can work in it.

If you want a self-hosted Todoist, pick **Vikunja**. If you want no server at all, pick **Super Productivity**. On Android, pick **Tasks.org**.

We build Open Sunsama, so we put it first — here is why, and where others fit better.

Most open source task managers stop at the list. Open Sunsama also helps you pick what fits today, then blocks time for it on your calendar.

Need an MIT or GPL license, a native phone app, or a list for chores at home? Other apps fit better, and we say which.

## Key takeaways

- **Best overall:** Open Sunsama, a to-do list and daily planner that works with Claude, ChatGPT and any MCP client.
- **Best self-hosted Todoist:** Vikunja, with list, kanban, Gantt and table views.
- **Best with no server:** Super Productivity, with apps for desktop, Android and iOS.
- **Lightest self-hosted app:** tududi, one Docker container with an MCP server built in.
- **Best on Android:** Tasks.org, synced to your own CalDAV server.
- **Best for a family:** Donetick, for shared chores that repeat and rotate.
- **Best on the Linux desktop:** Planify, or Errands for something smaller.
- **Best in the terminal:** Taskwarrior, or todo.txt for one plain text file.
- **Licenses differ.** Most apps here use MIT, GPL or AGPL. Open Sunsama uses a non-commercial license.

## How we compared these apps

We build Open Sunsama and use it every day to plan our own work. For every other app, we read the license file, the release history, the docs and the issue tracker. We pulled stars and releases from GitHub on 24 Sep 2026.

We captured the screenshots ourselves from each app's demo, web app or official images. The line under each screenshot says where it came from.

We did not use every app for weeks, so we don't claim to have tested them. An app made the list only if it is open source and still gets fixes. When an app is slow to change, we say so.

## 1. Open Sunsama is the best open source to-do app for planning your day

<OssApp id="open-sunsama" rank={1} bestFor="People who want their to-do list, calendar and AI agent in one place." />

**Verdict: best overall.** It is a to-do list that also plans your day, so you stop carrying a wall of tasks from one week to the next.

Each day is a column on a board, next to your backlog. You drag what fits into Today. Tasks you don't finish roll over to tomorrow.

Then you drag a task onto the calendar to block time for it. Your Google, Outlook or iCloud events sit next to your blocks, so you see how much time you really have.

- **Good:** subtasks, notes, file attachments, priorities P0 to P3, recurring tasks, reminders, and Cmd+K to jump anywhere.
- **Good:** a public REST API and a hosted MCP server, so Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor can add and move tasks for you.
- **Good:** self-host it with Docker, or use the desktop apps for Mac, Windows and Linux.
- **Falls short:** there is no iOS or Android app. On a phone, you use the mobile web app.
- **Falls short:** it has no Todoist importer, and it plans one person's day, not a team's projects.
- **Pick it if** you want a calm, realistic day, not just a longer list.

## 2. Vikunja is the closest thing to a self-hosted Todoist

<OssApp id="vikunja" rank={2} bestFor="Todoist users who want the same workflow on their own server." />

**Verdict:** the safest self-hosted to-do app. It is mature and busy, and it shows the same list as a list, a kanban board, a Gantt chart or a table.

- **Good:** it imports from Todoist, TickTick, Trello and Microsoft To Do.
- **Good:** CalDAV, reminders, lists you share with other people, a REST API and webhooks.
- **Falls short:** the Android app is still an alpha, and there is no iOS app. Most people use the web app on their phone.
- **Falls short:** its MCP server is on the main branch but not yet in a stable release.
- **Pick it if** you are leaving Todoist and want lists your family or team can share.

Our [open source Todoist alternatives](/blog/open-source-todoist-alternatives) guide compares it with Todoist in more depth.

## 3. Super Productivity is the best to-do app with no server at all

<OssApp id="super-productivity" rank={3} bestFor="People who want a local app on every device, with time tracking built in." />

**Verdict:** the best MIT-licensed to-do app for one person. It works offline, needs no account, and runs on every desktop and phone.

- **Good:** apps for Linux, macOS, Windows, Android and iOS, plus a web app.
- **Good:** timeboxing, a Pomodoro timer, time tracking and break reminders.
- **Good:** it pulls in issues from Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Linear and more, and it can import from Todoist.
- **Falls short:** it is built for one person. Sync goes through WebDAV, Dropbox or SuperSync, not a shared server.
- **Falls short:** MCP comes from community servers, not the core app.
- **Pick it if** you want an app that works offline and tracks where your time goes.

## 4. tududi is the lightest self-hosted to-do app, with MCP built in

<OssApp id="tududi" rank={4} bestFor="Home-labbers who want tasks, projects and notes in one small container." />

**Verdict:** the simplest server to run. One container holds your areas, projects, tasks and notes.

- **Good:** Today, Upcoming and Someday views, plus recurring tasks that can repeat from the day you finish them.
- **Good:** CalDAV sync with apps like Tasks.org, a Telegram bot for quick capture, and an MCP server in every install.
- **Falls short:** there is no native phone app. You add the web app to your home screen.
- **Falls short:** there is no calendar view, so you can't block time for a task.
- **Pick it if** you want a small list your AI agent can use today.

## 5. Tasks.org is the best open source to-do app for Android

<OssApp id="tasks-org" rank={5} bestFor="Android users who want reminders that fire, synced to a server they control." />

**Verdict:** the most complete open source to-do app on Android. It grew out of Astrid, a popular app that shut down in 2013.

- **Good:** tags, filters, lists, subtasks as deep as you like, widgets and many reminder options.
- **Good:** it syncs with your own CalDAV server, DAVx⁵, EteSync or Google Tasks. Pair it with Nextcloud, Radicale or tududi.
- **Falls short:** there is no iOS app, and the desktop app is still early.
- **Pick it if** your phone runs Android and you want your tasks off Google.

A native app matters here. Self-hosters often say reminders are the weak spot of web-based to-do apps, and a phone app fires them on time.

## 6. Donetick is the best to-do app for shared chores at home

<OssApp id="donetick" rank={6} bestFor="Families and roommates who share chores that repeat." />

**Verdict:** the best self-hosted list for a household. It is built for chores that come back, and for the people who share them.

- **Good:** you type a task in plain English, like "take the trash out every Monday at 6:15 pm", and it sets the repeat.
- **Good:** it rotates chores between people and gives points for done tasks.
- **Good:** a Home Assistant integration, OIDC login, a REST API and webhooks. Alerts go to Telegram, Discord or Pushover.
- **Falls short:** the phone apps are early: an Android APK and an iOS test build on TestFlight.
- **Falls short:** it is made for chores, not for planning a work day.
- **Pick it if** you want one shared list for your home that you run yourself.

## 7. Planify is the best to-do app for the Linux desktop

<OssApp id="planify" rank={7} bestFor="GNOME users who want a polished native app, synced with Todoist or Nextcloud." />

**Verdict:** the most polished to-do app on the Linux desktop. It looks and feels like a modern GNOME app.

- **Good:** drag and drop, sections, labels, several reminders per task, recurring tasks and a calendar view to plan your day.
- **Good:** it syncs with Todoist, Nextcloud or CalDAV, or stays local. A small command-line tool can add and list tasks.
- **Falls short:** it runs on Linux only, from Flathub. There is no phone or web app.
- **Pick it if** you live on the GNOME desktop and want a native app.

## 8. Nextcloud Tasks is the best pick if you already run Nextcloud

<OssApp id="nextcloud-tasks" rank={8} bestFor="Nextcloud users who want tasks next to their files and calendar." />

**Verdict:** the easy choice for Nextcloud users. Your tasks live on your own server as standard CalDAV to-dos.

- **Good:** any CalDAV app can read them, such as Tasks.org, Apple Reminders or Thunderbird.
- **Good:** subtasks, due dates, priorities, and lists you can share with other Nextcloud users.
- **Falls short:** you need a whole Nextcloud server. Recent work on the app is mostly upkeep.
- **Pick it if** Nextcloud is already your home for files, contacts and your calendar.

## 9. Taskwarrior is the best to-do app for the terminal

<OssApp id="taskwarrior" rank={9} bestFor="Developers who live in the terminal and want reports and scripts." />

**Verdict:** the most capable to-do list for the command line. It has been around for years and still ships new releases.

- **Good:** tags, projects, priorities, due dates and an urgency score that sorts your list for you.
- **Good:** hooks, lots of add-on tools, and an optional sync server called TaskChampion.
- **Falls short:** there is no official phone or web app, and Windows needs WSL.
- **Falls short:** you learn a small command language before it pays off.
- **Pick it if** you want your list one command away.

## 10. Errands is the simplest to-do list for GNOME

<OssApp id="errands" rank={10} bestFor="Linux users who want a small, clean list with sub-tasks." />

**Verdict:** a small, calm list for the Linux desktop. It is part of GNOME Circle, a set of well-made GNOME apps.

- **Good:** sub-tasks, several lists, drag and drop and a color for each task.
- **Good:** it syncs with Nextcloud Tasks over CalDAV.
- **Falls short:** the README says only Nextcloud is known to work well for sync.
- **Falls short:** stable releases are slow while a rewrite is under way. See the card for the latest one.
- **Pick it if** Planify feels like more than you need.

## 11. todo.txt keeps your whole list in one plain text file

<OssApp id="todo-txt" rank={11} bestFor="People who want a list they can read, search and sync with anything." />

**Verdict:** the most future-proof list there is. Your tasks are lines in a text file, and a shell script manages them.

- **Good:** a simple format: (A) for priority, +project for a project and @context for a place or tool.
- **Good:** many other apps read the same file, on desktop and phone, so you are never locked in.
- **Falls short:** no reminders and no sync of its own. You sync the file with a tool you already use.
- **Pick it if** you want a list that will still open in 20 years.

## MIT, GPL and AGPL all let you self-host for yourself; non-commercial adds a limit for companies

The license tells you what you may do with the code. For one person self-hosting an app, MIT, GPL and AGPL all give you full freedom. Their rules only kick in when you share the app, or a changed copy, with other people.

| License | Apps in this list | Self-host for yourself | Change the code | Use at a company |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MIT** | Super Productivity, tududi, Taskwarrior, Errands | Yes | Yes. Keep the copyright notice. | Yes |
| **GPL-3.0** | Tasks.org, Planify, todo.txt | Yes | Yes. If you give the app to others, share your changes too. | Yes |
| **AGPL-3.0** | Vikunja, Donetick, Nextcloud Tasks | Yes | Yes. If others use your changed copy, even over the web, share your changes. | Yes |
| **Non-commercial** | Open Sunsama | Yes, for personal use | Yes, for personal use | Needs a commercial license |

AGPL closes a gap in the GPL. With the GPL, you only share changes when you hand out the app itself. With AGPL, running a changed copy as a website for others counts too.

Here is Open Sunsama's license in plain words. The code is public on GitHub, under a non-commercial license.

You can read it, run it and self-host it for personal use. Companies need a commercial license.

That is not an OSI license like MIT or GPL. If your company needs one, pick Vikunja, tududi or Super Productivity. Also note that a few open source apps sell extras, like Vikunja Pro or a Tasks.org subscription.

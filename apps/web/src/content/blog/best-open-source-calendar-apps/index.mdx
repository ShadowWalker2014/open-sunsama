The best open source calendar app for planning your day is **Open Sunsama**. It puts your tasks and your Google, Outlook or iCloud calendar in one day view, with time blocking and any AI agent. It plans on top of the calendar you already have, so it does not replace a calendar server.

To host the calendar itself, pick **Nextcloud Calendar** for a full self-hosted Google Calendar, or **Radicale** for the lightest server. Then add **Thunderbird** on desktop and **Fossify Calendar** with **DAVx⁵** on Android.

A calendar has two halves: the server where your events live, and the app you look at. Google gives you both, so leaving Google means picking one of each.

## A calendar server stores events, and calendar apps show them

Most "open source calendar" lists mix up two kinds of software. That is why the same list can hold Radicale and Thunderbird, which do very different jobs.

A **calendar server** is where your events live. Nextcloud Calendar, Radicale and Baïkal are servers you run yourself. Your phone and laptop sync with it over CalDAV, the open standard for calendar sync.

A **calendar app** is what you look at. Thunderbird, GNOME Calendar, Fossify Calendar and Etar are apps. They can show events from Google, from your own server, or from both at once.

Some tools sit on top of both. Open Sunsama is a planner that shows your calendar next to your tasks. Cal.diy is a booking page that reads your calendar to find free times.

## How to replace Google Calendar with open source

You need three things: a server to hold your events, a way to sync, and an app on each device. Here is the path most self-hosters take.

1. **Pick a server.** Nextcloud Calendar if you want a web calendar and sharing. Radicale or Baïkal if you only need sync. If you don't want to run a server, Proton or Tuta host it for you.
2. **Put it behind HTTPS.** Use your own domain and a reverse proxy, then back up the data folder every night. Our [self-hosted productivity guide](/blog/self-hosted-productivity-apps) covers both.
3. **Export from Google.** In Google Calendar, open **Settings**, then **Import & export**, then **Export**. You get a .zip with one .ics file per calendar.
4. **Import your events.** In Nextcloud Calendar, use **Import calendar** in its settings. For Radicale or Baïkal, add the server to Thunderbird, then use **Events and Tasks**, then **Import**.
5. **Connect your phone.** On Android, install DAVx⁵, then view events in Fossify Calendar or Etar. On an iPhone, add a CalDAV account in Settings, under Calendar accounts.
6. **Connect your computer.** Thunderbird works on Windows, Mac and Linux. GNOME Calendar and the Mac's own Calendar app can add CalDAV accounts too.
7. **Plan your days.** Keep a calendar for meetings, and a planner for the work between them.

One honest note on step 7. Open Sunsama connects to Google, Outlook and iCloud today. Its iCloud form takes a custom CalDAV URL, but those three are the calendars we support.

## Open source licenses in plain words

Every app here shows its license on its card. Here is what those licenses mean for you.

| License | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **MIT** (Cal.diy) | Use, change and share the code for anything. |
| **GPL-3.0** (Radicale, Baïkal, Fossify, Etar, DAVx⁵, GNOME Calendar, Proton, Tuta) | Use and change it freely. If you share a changed version, share your code too. |
| **AGPL-3.0** (Nextcloud Calendar) | Like GPL, and it also covers apps you run for others over the web. |
| **MPL-2.0** (Thunderbird) | Share changes to its own files; the rest of your project can use any license. |
| **Non-commercial** (Open Sunsama) | Read it, run it and self-host it for personal use. Companies need a commercial license. |

For personal use and self-hosting, all of these give you full control. Open Sunsama's license is not an OSI license like MIT or GPL, so check it if you are a company.

## Which calendars work with AI agents?

An AI agent talks to apps over MCP. Here is where each calendar stands.

- **Open Sunsama:** a hosted MCP server with OAuth sign-in. Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor can read your meetings and plan time blocks around them. Calendar events are read-only over MCP. See the [MCP setup guide](/docs/mcp/overview).
- **Nextcloud Calendar:** Nextcloud's Context Agent app has an MCP server with calendar tools. It needs the AppAPI setup.
- **Radicale and Baïkal:** community CalDAV MCP servers work with any CalDAV server.
- **DAVx⁵:** its makers publish davmcp, an early MCP server for CalDAV events. Its own README says it is not ready for real use.
- **Proton and Tuta:** no public calendar API, so only small community projects exist, or none.

- **For most people: Open Sunsama.** Keep your calendar where it is, and plan your tasks right next to your meetings.
- **If you want Google Calendar on your own server,** pick Nextcloud Calendar.
- **If you want the smallest possible server,** pick Radicale, or Baïkal on a PHP host.

- **If you use Android,** pick DAVx⁵ plus Fossify Calendar.
- **If you want privacy but no server,** pick Proton Calendar or Tuta Calendar.
- **If you want booking links,** pick Cal.diy.
